# Price the work that stays with people

The illustrative model returns $10,000 in net monthly saving and 11.81 months to payback for one role. Those are Av’s model outputs, not a deployment benchmark. Agent count is not the financial case. Avoidable cost after the human queue is.

Use this calculator before approving a replacement budget. Enter exit cost per role, roles cut, avoidable loaded annual cost, total annual agent run cost and one-off implementation cost. Then price the work still routed to people. Supply either full-workload annual human staffing cost or loaded cost per unit and annual workload. The calculator applies the residual share once.

The exit-cost example uses HubSpot’s October 6, 2026 Form 8-K and its incorporated employee update. Av’s own derivation divides estimated charges of $65–75 million by nearly 660 exits: roughly $98,000–$114,000 per exit. That is an aggregate charge proxy, not individual severance. HubSpot explicitly said the change was not driven by AI-related efficiencies. Every other example input is illustrative input, not a benchmark.

Run the supplied CLI. Read net monthly saving, payback months and the 24-month net position together. Compare the residual-share scenarios. A nonpositive monthly saving means no payback. A positive saving can still leave the investment underwater at the horizon.

Build the output into the kit’s approval worksheet. This is an undiscounted, steady-state screen. It does not price rollout delays, fixed staffing floors or lost service quality. Measure residual routing and rework before approving cuts.

The CFO owns the payback line. Operations owns evidence that the remaining capacity can deliver. HR owns the exit obligations. Approve the deployment only when all three reconcile.
