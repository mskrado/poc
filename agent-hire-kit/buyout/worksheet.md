# For the person leaving: buyout decision worksheet

*Templates, not legal advice.*

This worksheet helps a worker who has been offered a voluntary buyout or severance, because agents now do part of the role, see what the offer pays and what it leaves to them. The HR lead making the offer can fill it in with the worker, so both sides price the same risks before anyone signs.

**A current example:** Microsoft's Voluntary Exit Offer, reported by Ashley Stewart in Business Insider on Oct 26, 2026, follows its US standard severance terms, which run up to 39 weeks of base pay depending on level and tenure.

## The worksheet

| Item | What to write down | Your figure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks of pay offered | The number of weeks, and the formula behind it (for example, weeks per year of service) | |
| Cash value | Weeks × weekly base pay. Note lump sum or installments, and the tax withheld | |
| Health cover | The date employer cover ends, and the monthly cost to continue it. US: COBRA premium. Canada: check your province's rules. EU: check your national system. Flag which applies | |
| Unvested equity or bonus | What vests, what is forfeited, and on what date | |
| Retirement match | The employer match you lose, and whether any unvested match is forfeited | |
| Release of claims | Does signing waive legal claims? US, age 40+: under the OWBPA/ADEA you get at least 21 days to consider (45 days in a group program) and 7 days to revoke after signing. Verify on eeoc.gov | |
| Unemployment benefits | Does taking a voluntary buyout change your eligibility, or delay payments? This varies by state. Ask your state unemployment agency | |
| Expected job-search length | Your own estimate, set against the benchmarks below | |
| Gap | Cash value minus (months of search × monthly costs, including health cover) | |

## Five checks before you sign

1. Get every term in writing, including the date each benefit ends.
2. Count the days you have to consider and to revoke, and mark both on a calendar.
3. Ask HR which group was eligible and how people were chosen.
4. Call your state unemployment agency and ask how a voluntary exit affects your claim.
5. Have an attorney or adviser of your choice read the release before the deadline.

## What the evidence says

The buyout pays for a number of weeks. The job search does not stop when the weeks run out.

- **Reemployment:** In January 2026, 66.1 percent of long-tenured displaced workers (those who had held their job for 3 years or more) were reemployed. For ages 55 to 64, the rate was 57.3 percent. Source: BLS, Worker Displacement: 2023–2025, Aug 27, 2026.
- **Pay after reemployment:** Of those who lost full-time jobs and were reemployed full-time, about 49 percent earned as much as or more than they did before, down from about 62 percent in January 2024. That means about half now earn less. Source: same BLS release.
- **Search length:** The median spell of unemployment was 11.9 weeks in October 2026 (seasonally adjusted), and 27.6 percent of unemployed people had been looking for 27 weeks or more. Source: BLS, Current Population Survey, Table A-12, Nov 6, 2026.

The displaced-worker survey covers people who lost or left jobs because a plant closed, work ran out or a position was abolished. It does not report buyouts separately, so treat these numbers as a benchmark, not a forecast for you.

For the HR lead: the worker carries the cost of the search, the health cover and the lower pay. Efficiency is not the same as welfare. A fair offer prices all three.
